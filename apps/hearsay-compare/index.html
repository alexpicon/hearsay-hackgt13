What does this game measure?

Your score is for this session and these demonstration clips only. Each pair contains exactly one synthetic recording, so random guessing averages 50%. That setup is different from screening an unknown recording. Speakers and clips may overlap training data; this is not a scientific test of you or HEARSAY.

The reveal uses dataset labels, not a HEARSAY prediction. Audio and labels are public; the game is for learning, not a secure exam.

A familiar voice is no longer enough

When a voice sounds right, a false story can travel.

A fabricated recording can impersonate a relative asking for money, a manager requesting a transfer, or a public figure saying something they never said. Voice synthesis also supports accessibility and creative work. The risk is deception: passing generated speech off as a real person's words.

Listen critically. Verify independently.

If a caller makes an urgent request, pause and contact the person using a number you already trust. FTC guidance on fake emergencies.

What a score can and cannot tell you

Higher scores lean synthetic. A score of 0.9 does not mean a 90% chance of being fake: these are decision scores, not calibrated probabilities. New generators, languages, background noise and encoding changes can shift performance.
